# UX Organization & Space Efficiency

- Keep UI and UX organized, scannable, and space-efficient.
- Never force the user to scroll to view a small set of items or controls (for example, 1–6 shop goods, menu options, or action buttons). Fit the entire set cleanly within the visible panel or viewport using compact headers, logical grouping, and responsive multi-column grids rather than tall single-column stacks.
- Reserve scrolling for genuinely long lists or prose; verify that compact modals and overlays have zero vertical overflow (`scrollHeight <= clientHeight`) on both desktop and mobile viewports.

# Anatomical, Biological & Spatial Realism

- Always apply anatomical, biological, and spatial common sense when designing characters, rigs, animations, props, and character-map interactions.
- Held props must sit physically inside and be gripped by the character's claw, fin, tentacle, or hand and be skin-weighted to that limb's bone — never floating in mid-air near a limb.
- Grounded creatures must rest solidly on surfaces (no hovering limbs or floor sinking); swimmers must float naturally in water; joints must bend only where biologically plausible without distorting unrelated torso, shell, or clothing meshes.
- Characters, stalls, and scenery props must never clip through each other or obstruct the encounter camera's sightline.
